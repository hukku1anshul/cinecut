# Veda lectures on YouTube: competitor analysis (September 2026)

Looked at: YouTube search results for "vedas explained", "upanishads explained", "वेद क्या है", "ऋग्वेद सूक्त अर्थ",
"gayatri mantra meaning", "nasadiya sukta explained", the channels behind the top results, and web recommendations.
View counts are as shown on YouTube on 2026-09-14.

## What the numbers say

1. People search for questions, and the one-video answers win: "वेद क्या है", "चारों वेदों में क्या लिखा है", "What exactly
   are the Vedas?" reach a million views, while a Hindi series's "Episode 1" has 33K. Every lesson must also stand alone as
   the best answer to a question people type.
2. Meaning with pronunciation is in constant demand: the Gayatri with its meaning and correct pronunciation, word by word,
   is among the most watched Vedic videos in both languages.
3. Relevance to life is what the biggest channels share (Acharya Prashant; The East Made Simple frames the Upanishads
   against anxiety and ego). The viewer asked for exactly this.
4. Long, chaptered videos do well when the chapters are clear (11 chapters on The East Made Simple; 7 on Vedic Discovery).
5. Shorts bring the reach: a verse and its meaning, or one surprising fact, gets 100K to millions of views.
6. Trust is rare: few channels show the Sanskrit on screen, cite the source, or say where tradition and scholarship differ;
   several descriptions are keyword-stuffed, and some titles overclaim.

## Where the existing channels are weak (our opening)

- No structured, bilingual course from the first question to the Upanishads, with one consistent format.
- Talks are long and unedited; overviews are one-off; sukta playlists are complete but dry.
- Little on-screen Sanskrit, almost no word-by-word meaning outside the Gayatri, and few sources.
- Hindi content often mixes in English and Urdu; pure, clear Hindi is rare.

## What we do about it

1. Titles that answer the question people search, with the lesson number after it:
   "What Is Actually in the Vedas? | Lesson 1"; "वेद क्या हैं, और चारों वेदों में क्या लिखा है? | पाठ 1". No overclaiming.
2. Every verse gets a word-by-word part (पद-अर्थ): each Sanskrit word written on the board with its meaning, then the whole
   verse again, recited slowly.
3. Every lesson has an "In your life" part (mottoes, prayers, ceremonies, and what the teaching can mean in an ordinary
   day), with a small thing to try that week; real people and events only when documented.
4. Lessons of 10-18 minutes with 8-12 clear chapters, each chapter title a small question.
5. A three-question quiz at the end; the answers open the next lesson (and are in the description).
6. Two or three Shorts cut from every lesson: the verse with its meaning; the most surprising fact; the life lesson.
   Each Short points to the full lesson.
7. Trust as a feature: the Sanskrit from Sanskrit Wikisource on screen, the translation credited, tradition and
   scholarship both given, and an honest note that the voice is AI.
8. One recognisable thumbnail template per course: the question in large type, a painting or manuscript, the lesson number.
9. Playlists by module, an end screen to the next lesson, and a steady schedule (one lesson a week in each language, Shorts
   in between).
10. Risk to watch: the Sanskrit is recited by an AI voice. Some viewers will prefer a traditional chanter; the note says so
    plainly, and a public-domain or openly licensed recording can be used where one exists.
